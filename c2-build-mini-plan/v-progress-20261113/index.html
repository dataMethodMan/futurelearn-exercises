        1. Draft the opportunity summary

        Port Coriol Labs reached out because its infrastructure limits experiment scaling and reliable application/data-pipeline integration. It wants more reliable lab workflows and predictable project costs before new academic partnerships begin within the next quarter.

        2. Capture stakeholder insights

        Chief technology officer: reported economic buyer, to confirm. Hypothesis: needs reliable scaling that supports new partnerships. Ask about approval authority and scaling criteria; compare a proposed technical approach with those criteria.

        Head of research operations: reported sponsor, to confirm. Hypothesis: wants fewer lab workflow delays. Ask which workflows lose time and what a useful improvement would look like; establish a baseline with the research team.

        Lab IT managers: reported technical influencers, to confirm. Hypothesis: need maintainable integrations and practical automation. Review the current application/data-pipeline dependencies and ask about implementation capacity, failure points and test evidence.

        Finance partner: reported budget approver, to confirm. Hypothesis: wants predictable costs and project-level spend visibility. Ask about approval limits, the budget cycle and the cost-allocation evidence needed to assess a proposed pilot.

        3. Formulate the value hypothesis

        Challenge: fragile integrations and limited automation constrain reliable experiment scaling, while project spend is hard to allocate.

        Outcome: fewer lab workflow delays and more predictable project costs as new partnerships begin.

        Approach: explore a limited integration-and-automation pilot with project-level cost reporting, after technical discovery establishes fit.

        Measurement: establish the baseline number of workflow delays per week and project spend visibility. Discuss an illustrative 20% delay-reduction target within the next quarter; validate feasibility and agree the target, pilot scope and comparison period with the customer before committing.

        Evidence: the case reports fragile integrations, limited automation and spend-allocation difficulty. It supplies no baseline, agreed target, pilot results or approved budget. Collect workflow and cost data and confirm success criteria before presenting the hypothesis as proven.

        4. Identify potential blockers

        Potential blocker: lab IT implementation capacity may be limited. Mitigation: ask about capacity and dependencies; if appropriate, agree a small pilot scope that fits the team's available effort.

        Potential blocker: budget approval or success criteria may remain unclear. Mitigation: confirm the finance approval process and agree baseline measures and review criteria before proposing a commitment.

        5. Define next actions

        Action 1: propose a discovery call with the chief technology officer, research operations and lab IT. Proposed owner: Ordinary Systems sales representative, with a technical colleague. Proposed timing: within one week, subject to customer agreement. Visible progress: a shared problem/priority record, reported roles checked, and a list of baseline data and approval questions.

        Action 2: share a draft small-pilot outline for operations, IT and finance review. Proposed owner: Ordinary Systems technical lead with the sales representative. Proposed timing: within two weeks, after discovery confirms feasibility and customer availability. Visible progress: proposed scope, effort, cost visibility and measures for customer feedback, with open approval conditions recorded.
