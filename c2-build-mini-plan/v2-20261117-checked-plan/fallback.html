Build a mini plan

Complete text version

Turn the supplied research about Port Coriol Labs into a five-section opportunity plan. Write a summary, capture stakeholder insights, form a value hypothesis, anticipate blockers and propose next actions.

This is ungraded planning practice. Each section includes a case-based decision with one correct answer. Correct that decision, fill the writing fields and review your reasoning before moving on. Incorrect choices show feedback and keep later sections locked. Written plans are self-reviewed; the activity does not automatically grade prose. Work and checked attempts save in this browser and device when storage is available. Restart clears this activity's saved work and attempts. Nothing is submitted.

Port Coriol Labs: supplied research

You are a sales representative for Ordinary Systems, a large company in the tech and cloud sector that specialises in enterprise software integration and information technology (IT) infrastructure management. You are working on an opportunity with Port Coriol Labs, a small startup that specialises in biotech research tools serving university labs and independent researchers.

They were referred to Ordinary Systems by a trusted collaborator. Port Coriol Labs reached out because their existing infrastructure is limiting their ability to scale experiments quickly, and they need more reliable integration between their research applications and data pipelines.

The background and research indicate that their leadership is under pressure to support new university partnerships, reduce downtime in lab workflows, and achieve better cost predictability. Early discovery suggests pains around fragile integrations, lack of automation, and difficulties allocating infrastructure spend to specific projects.

The buying group appears to include a chief technology officer (CTO), described as economic buyer; a head of research operations, described as sponsor; lab IT managers, described as technical influencers; and a finance partner, described as budget approver. Confirm the roles and decision process in discovery.

Timing is sensitive, as new academic partnerships are scheduled to start within the next quarter. This is the fictional case's timeframe, not a date calculated from today.

Evidence guidance

Keep case facts, your hypotheses and your proposed actions distinct. The case reports early discovery and a buying group that appears to include these roles; it does not establish an approved budget, agreed solution, success baseline or complete approval process.

The template reading's business problem corresponds to Challenge; proposed outcome corresponds to Outcome. Keep Evidence visible: what is supported now, and what proof is still needed? Approach describes a proposed method; Measurement describes how you would validate the result.

The source example's 20% reduction in lab delays is an illustrative proposed target, not a verified result or customer agreement. Establish a baseline, validate feasibility and agree a target and review period before treating any number as a commitment.

Proposed next actions need an owner, a time window and a useful visible output. Label these as your plan; the scenario does not say the customer has accepted meetings, pilots or deadlines.
