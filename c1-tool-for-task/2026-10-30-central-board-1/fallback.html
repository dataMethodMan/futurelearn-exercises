Audience: Customer-facing. Purpose: Tell a business story live. Tool: Pitch deck.

A pitch deck tells a focused story that connects the client's problem to your solution. Its typical sections are a company overview, the customer challenge, a solution overview and proof points. For a cautious leadership team, the customer challenge slide puts the slow platform in business terms, and the proof points show results rather than features. That is what an introductory meeting needs to capture attention.

Where in the sale is this? The opening presentation, after discovery and before the client has heard your solution.

What information do you need? The client's challenge framed in business terms, a relevant solution and documented evidence, if available, to support the proposed value. Scalability and reliability are capabilities you have explored this week.

Who will see it? The client's leadership team, live in the meeting.

Audience: Customer-facing. Purpose: Share a concise follow-up. Tool: One-pager.

A one-pager reinforces key messages after a meeting. It typically includes a headline and value statement, relevant features, supporting evidence if available, and a call to action. Here it can summarise how proposed capabilities relate to the client's goals without promising outcomes that have not been established. A single page is easy to share with senior management, as Eli Chen demonstrated with his follow-up one-pager.

Where in the sale is this? Straight after the first presentation, before the client agrees to the next meeting.

What information do you need? The main message in a form the operations director can pass to people who were not in the room.

Who will see it? The operations director first, then senior management inside the client's organisation.

Audience: Internal. Purpose: Prepare responses to concerns. Tool: Objection-handling guide.

This guide lists common concerns with recommended responses, supporting evidence and follow-up prompts. It helps you prepare a consistent explanation and identify what still needs checking for this customer. You saw this preparation role in the preceding guide example.

Where in the sale is this? A second consultation, where the client's concerns are the focus.

Who will see it? Your team during preparation; the client hears your explanation.
