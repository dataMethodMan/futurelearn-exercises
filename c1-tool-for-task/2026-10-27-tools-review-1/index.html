    Build and revise your preparation plan

    On paper or in a document, make four entries: task, audience, purpose, tool and reason. For each task, answer: Where in the sale is this? What information do you need? Who will see it? Choose an audience, purpose and tool from the lists below. Check both constraints using the tool descriptions. Then compare with the task feedback. If a constraint fails, revise before adding the entry to your plan. You may reuse tools; no resource quota applies. Save or copy your own rationale if you want to keep it.

    Task 1: Opening the first presentation

    You are preparing your first presentation to a client's leadership team, after the discovery call. The leadership team is cautious about adopting new technology and hesitant to commit without clear business outcomes. The company's online platform slows down during peak shopping events. You need to make a strong impression by connecting cloud benefits, such as scalability and reliability, to measurable business results rather than technical features.

    What information do you need?

    Which tool do you prepare for this meeting?

    • One-pager - Customer-facing
    • Objection-handling guide - Internal
    • Product quick-reference sheet - Internal

    Compare the brief with your thinking

    Where in the sale is this? The opening presentation, after discovery and before the client has heard your solution.

    What information do you need? The client's challenge framed in business terms, a relevant solution and documented evidence, if available, to support the proposed value. Scalability and reliability are capabilities you have explored this week.

    Who will see it? The client's leadership team, live in the meeting.

    Audience: Customer-facing. Purpose: Tell a business story live.

    Pitch deck: Fits this task. A pitch deck tells a focused story that connects the client's problem to your solution. Its typical sections are a company overview, the customer challenge, a solution overview and proof points. For a cautious leadership team, the customer challenge slide puts the slow platform in business terms, and the proof points show results rather than features. That is what an introductory meeting needs to capture attention.

    One-pager: Not the fit for this task. A one-pager is a follow-up tool. It summarises benefits on a single written page, with a value statement, key features and a call to action. It reinforces a conversation that has already happened, so it comes after this meeting.

    Objection-handling guide: Not the fit for this task. This guide helps you prepare company-recommended responses to common concerns such as cost or security. The main task here is to introduce a business story to the leadership team.

    Product quick-reference sheet: Not the fit for this task. This sheet helps you check product details such as uptime information or pricing. This meeting calls for a business story rather than a list of technical specifications.

    • Objection-handling guide - Internal
    • Product quick-reference sheet - Internal

    Compare the brief with your thinking

    Where in the sale is this? Straight after the first presentation, before the client agrees to the next meeting.

    What information do you need? The main message in a form the operations director can pass to people who were not in the room.

    Who will see it? The operations director first, then senior management inside the client's organisation.

    Audience: Customer-facing. Purpose: Share a concise follow-up.

    Pitch deck: Not the fit for this task. A pitch deck is used during a live presentation. It carries the full story, and it needs you there to present it. The operations director needs something shorter that works without you.

    One-pager: Fits this task. A one-pager reinforces key messages after a meeting. It typically includes a headline and value statement, relevant features, supporting evidence if available, and a call to action. Here it can summarise how proposed capabilities relate to the client's goals without promising outcomes that have not been established. A single page is easy to share with senior management, as Eli Chen demonstrated with his follow-up one-pager.

    Objection-handling guide: Not the fit for this task. This guide is organised by concern and recommended response. Its main role is to prepare your team for questions; it is not the concise customer-facing summary requested here.

    Product quick-reference sheet: Not the fit for this task. This sheet is used internally to confirm technical details before a meeting. It does not summarise benefits for a client to share.

    Compare the brief with your thinking

    Where in the sale is this? A second consultation, where the client's concerns are the focus.

    What information do you need? Company-recommended responses, supporting evidence and questions to clarify concerns about cost control, data protection, migration and compliance. Distinguish what is supported from what still needs checking.

    Who will see it? Your team during preparation; the client hears your explanation.

    Audience: Internal. Purpose: Prepare responses to concerns.

    Pitch deck: Not the fit for this task. A pitch deck gives an overview of the solution and includes success stories. It does not hold approved responses to specific concerns.

    One-pager: Not the fit for this task. A one-pager highlights benefits such as speed, cost efficiency or compliance. It does not set out specific client concerns or how to answer them.

    Objection-handling guide: Fits this task. This guide lists common concerns with recommended responses, supporting evidence and follow-up prompts. It helps you prepare a consistent explanation and identify what still needs checking for this customer. You saw this preparation role in the preceding guide example.

    Product quick-reference sheet: Not the fit for this task. This sheet summarises product features and service details. It helps you stay accurate, but it does not address client concerns or give you approved language for answering them.

    Where in the sale is this? A technical meeting, with time only for a last check.

    What information do you need? Specific product details: documented uptime commitments, if any, and relevant data centre locations.

    Who will see it? You use the reference to prepare an answer for the IT manager.

    Audience: Internal. Purpose: Check specific product facts.

    Pitch deck: Not the fit for this task. A pitch deck highlights benefits and business value. It does not hold technical detail at the level the IT manager is asking for.

    One-pager: Not the fit for this task. A one-pager summarises client benefits on one page. It does not contain detailed service figures.

    Objection-handling guide: Not the fit for this task. This guide prepares you to answer concerns. These are factual questions, and the guide is not where you verify specifications.

    Product quick-reference sheet: Fits this task. This internal summary helps you locate service details such as documented uptime commitments, data centre locations and pricing. It is useful for a quick product-fact check; if a detail is missing or unclear, identify what needs confirmation rather than guess.
