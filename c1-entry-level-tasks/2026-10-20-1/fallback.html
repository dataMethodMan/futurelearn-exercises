Who should pick this up? - complete text version

Follow work across the complete B2B sales team. Match each scenario to the role that should lead it: sales development representative, account executive, solution architect or customer success manager. Then choose two items for a priority plan, inspect the handovers you leave uncovered, and revise when the priority changes.

Choose a priority. For every work item, select the role that should lead it and decide whether to include it in your two-item plan. Review the plan, revise any gaps, then try a changed priority. Finish by writing and saving a short rationale. Allow about 5–7 minutes.

For this planning exercise, your plan has two task slots. This is an exercise constraint, not an estimate of task duration or a rule for a real working day.

Choose the role that should lead each scenario. Other roles may support the work in practice, but one role owns the next action at this point in the customer journey.

Qualify a new lead

A new contact is interested in Jin Cloud Solutions. Research the company, make first contact and check whether there is genuine interest, a suitable budget and decision-making authority before a handover.

Sales development representative (SDR): This is the most direct match: the SDR researches, contacts and qualifies the lead before handover.

Account executive (AE): The account executive takes over once the opportunity is qualified. This item is still at the SDR stage.

Solution architect (SA): Technical design comes later. The immediate need is to decide whether this lead is worth pursuing.

Customer success manager (CSM): Customer success works after the sale. This contact has not yet become a qualified opportunity.

Most direct match: Sales development representative (SDR). The SDR owns the front of the pipeline: finding, nurturing and qualifying potential customers before handing a real opportunity to the account executive.

Shape the commercial opportunity

A qualified prospect needs deeper discovery. Clarify its security needs, compliance requirements, budget and timeline, then turn those findings into a proposal and negotiate the agreement.

Sales development representative (SDR): The lead is already qualified. The SDR hands it over rather than owning proposal and negotiation.

Account executive (AE): This is the most direct match: the account executive leads discovery, the proposal and the commercial agreement.

Solution architect (SA): The solution architect supports technical fit, but the account executive owns the commercial opportunity.

Customer success manager (CSM): Customer success takes responsibility after the sale, not during proposal and negotiation.

Most direct match: Account executive (AE). The account executive owns the qualified commercial opportunity: discovery, the business case, the proposal and negotiation, bringing in specialist support when needed.

Design the technical fit

The prospect needs the proposed cloud platform to integrate with existing systems and meet security and compliance requirements. Assess feasibility and translate the needs into a clear technical roadmap.

Sales development representative (SDR): The lead is well beyond qualification. This item needs technical expertise rather than SDR ownership.

Account executive (AE): The account executive owns the deal and works alongside this role, but the technical roadmap belongs to the solution architect.

Solution architect (SA): This is the most direct match: the solution architect assesses feasibility and designs the technical fit.

Customer success manager (CSM): Customer success may help after deployment, but the solution is still being designed before the sale.

Most direct match: Solution architect (SA). The solution architect owns technical fit: assessing requirements, shaping the solution and explaining how it will work securely with the customer's systems.

Drive adoption and value

The contract is signed and the solution has been deployed. Guide the customer's team through onboarding and training, monitor usage, resolve adoption issues and identify ways to increase long-term value.

Sales development representative (SDR): This is now a customer, not a lead. The SDR's qualification work finished before the sale.

Account executive (AE): The commercial agreement is signed. The account executive hands the customer into post-sale success.

Solution architect (SA): The solution architect may support technical issues, but ongoing adoption and value are owned by customer success.

Customer success manager (CSM): This is the most direct match: the customer success manager leads onboarding, adoption and long-term value.

Most direct match: Customer success manager (CSM). The customer success manager owns the post-sale relationship: onboarding, adoption, value realisation, renewals and opportunities to deepen the partnership.
