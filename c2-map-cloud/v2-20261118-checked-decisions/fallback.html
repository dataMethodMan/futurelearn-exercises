The conceptual map, in full

Abstract teaching diagram, not a real provider map or the customer's architecture. Area A contains three separated facility groups: B, B2 and B3. Area D contains D1, D2 and D3. Point C is a separate content-delivery location near a group of users. Counts and placement are illustrative.

Area A contains facility groups B, B2 and B3. Area D contains D1, D2 and D3. Point C is separate and represents suitable content delivery near users. These are illustrative relationships, not the customer's deployment.

The Region, Availability zone and Edge location buttons highlight the outer containers, separated groups and Point C respectively. Their full explanations follow.

Region

A region is a geographic area that contains separate availability zones in this model. Location choice can affect user proximity and data-location needs; confirm the relevant service options and requirements. Area A and Area D are different regions here.

Availability zone

An availability zone is a separated group of one or more data centres within a region. Multiple zones offer different physical fault boundaries. Applications and data need an appropriate design and service configuration to use that separation; they do not move automatically just because zones exist.

Edge location

An edge location can bring suitable content delivery closer to users. Cached content may be served there, while a cache miss or a dynamic request may still need the origin. It is not another full region or a universal copy of the customer's database.

The buyer asks: What happens to our data if one facility fails?
