        Whose concern is it? - complete text version

        You have finished a first call about a proposed cloud-based analytics platform for Klatch Mercantile, a fictional importer of artisanal food and drink. Match each concern to the given buying-team responsibility it most directly addresses, so you can prepare a useful response. In a real call, several people may share a concern; a question alone does not prove who has approval authority.

        Work through four short stages. Match five notes to the given case responsibilities, compare two reasons for the rollout concern, match four more notes without the reminder card, then write a useful customer relationship management (CRM) note. Use the stated case responsibilities rather than assuming a concern belongs exclusively to a permanent buyer type.

        The company and the people are fictional. This activity is not scored, and nothing you choose or write is stored or sent anywhere unless you save a text file yourself. Allow about 10 minutes.

        How to use this version

        You can do every stage on paper or in a document. Read each note and match it to the given responsibility it most directly addresses, then read the feedback for each option and compare it with your choice. For the rollout note, select the two responsibilities explicitly described. If you chose differently, note why the feedback disagrees and try the note again. Work through the stages in order, as in the interactive version. After two unsuccessful tries on a note, read its correct answer and the reasoning.

        Stage 2 of 4: Same concern, different reasons

        The rollout note calls for two kinds of review: financial effects and secure integration. Match each given responsibility to its reason. Other people may also be affected by disruption.

        Why would David raise this concern?

        A disruption can mean financial losses and lower productivity while the rollout is under way. - Correct. Feedback: That fits. David’s assigned task is to review financial effects, so interrupted trading matters through lost income and productivity.

        A disruption would make the platform harder for people to learn. - Not the best fit. Feedback: This describes learning effort, which is Mei’s given responsibility. It does not explain the financial-loss review explicitly assigned to David in this note.

        A disruption could slow the launch of new services. - Not the best fit. Feedback: This describes product-growth timing, which is Sofia’s given responsibility. It does not explain the financial-loss review assigned to David here.

        Why would Markus raise this concern?

        He wants operations to stay stable and the platform to connect to existing systems smoothly and with little risk. - Correct. Feedback: That fits. Markus’s assigned task is to assess security and integration risk, so stable, safe connections are the reason given for his review.

        He wants to be sure the rollout stays within budget. - Not the best fit. Feedback: This is the financial-review reason assigned to David in the note. Markus may care about budget too, but the stated reason for his review here is secure integration.

        He wants every user to find the platform easy from the first day. - Not the best fit. Feedback: This is the learning-and-usability responsibility assigned to Mei. The stated reason for Markus’s review here is secure integration.

        One rollout issue can need several responses. Record the particular reason and the evidence behind it, rather than treating a role label as proof of everything a person cares about.
