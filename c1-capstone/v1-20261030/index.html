The customer case

Company and customers

Sto Lat Select is a fictional, mid-sized e-commerce retailer founded in 2016. It sells lifestyle and home goods, including home decor, small appliances and personalised accessories. It has about 250 employees, no physical shops, and customers in the United States, Canada and parts of Western Europe. It plans to expand internationally.

How it operates

All sales go through its website. A small engineering team maintains on-premises servers and connects the storefront to email marketing, customer analytics, payments, inventory and order-management tools. Marketing uses personalised recommendations, email campaigns, seasonal promotions and social advertising. Customer support uses separate chat, tracking and feedback systems.

Peak traffic

Promotions, holiday shopping and influencer campaigns cause traffic spikes. The current infrastructure cannot increase capacity quickly. The case reports slower pages, checkout delays, occasional outages and additional work on patching and capacity management. The case does not quantify the frequency, cost or duration of those problems.

Customer information

Customer data is spread across five or more systems. Profiles are inconsistent, staff make manual exports, and personalisation is limited. Leadership wants a clearer view of customers and buying behaviour. Teams also find it difficult to report on customer value, repeat purchases, product recommendations and inventory needs.

Growth and protection

International expansion brings additional data-protection, payment-security and regional compliance questions. The team is concerned about patching, access controls, sensitive data and audit records. Cloud adoption alone would not establish compliance; the team would need to investigate responsibilities and controls.

Priorities and unknowns

Over the next 12–18 months, leadership wants more reliable website performance, better customer insights, less maintenance work and support for safe growth. It is exploring cloud options but has not started a formal evaluation. The case gives no confirmed budget, decision maker, migration deadline, agreed service choice or measured savings. Treat those as questions, not facts.

Profile the customer â€” worked example

Sto Lat Select is an online-only retailer of lifestyle and home goods with about 250 employees. Its website and digital marketing support all sales, so the traffic-related delays and occasional outages described in the case are relevant discovery topics.

Its customer information is spread across several systems and reporting requires manual work. Leadership is exploring cloud options to support growth, customer insights and more reliable operations. Budget, decision authority and a migration deadline have not yet been confirmed.

Identify possible cloud needs â€” worked example

1. Website reliability during promotions: capacity that can adjust with demand and suitable availability options may help. Confirm traffic patterns, the application's constraints and acceptable interruption before making a recommendation.

2. Customer information and reporting: integration and analytics capabilities may help teams use consistent information. Confirm data ownership, quality, access and the reports people need.

3. Maintenance and protection: managed services, access controls and monitoring may support the engineering team. Clarify responsibilities, current effort and requirements; neither lower costs nor compliance is established by moving to cloud services.

Prepare the conversation â€” worked example

What happens for customers and your team when a promotion causes a traffic spike? How do different teams use customer information today, and where do inconsistencies cause the most difficulty? Which improvement would matter most over the next year, and how would you judge its success? What concerns would you want addressed before evaluating a change to your current systems? Who would need to be involved in discussing the priorities, budget and next steps?

Choose useful sales materials â€” worked example

Customer engagement cloud one-pager: prepare a customer-facing overview linking reliability and better customer information to possible capabilities. It can help stakeholders frame questions without presenting an approved solution.

Cloud migration objection-handling guide: use internally to prepare for concerns about disruption, cost and data protection. It should help the team plan a technical follow-up, not promise that concerns have been resolved.

Analytics and data capabilities quick-reference sheet: use internally to check terminology and prepare questions about fragmented customer information and reporting. Confirm any service-specific detail before sharing it.

Company: Sto Lat Select, an online-only home and lifestyle retailer with about 250 employees. Needs / Challenges: peak-traffic delays and outages; inconsistent customer information; manual reporting; growing protection and maintenance demands. Unknowns: impact measures, budget, decision authority, required controls and any migration deadline. Internal alignment: prepare discovery questions about priorities and current systems; involve a technical colleague when requirements are clearer. Possible direction: investigate elastic capacity, data integration and analytics, and managed-service options. No provider or architecture has been agreed. Next step: prepare a discovery meeting and confirm who should attend. Agree responsibility and timing with the team rather than inventing customer commitments.
