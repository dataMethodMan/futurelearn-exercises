Build a buyer persona from evidence - complete text version

You are preparing for a discovery call with a data analyst at Ordinary Systems, a fictional technology company. Use Carla’s fictional case from a similar company to build a practice persona and plan questions. At work, check a persona against customer research and what your contact tells you.

You will read the case, then build the persona one section at a time: who they are, what they care about, their pain points, how to engage them and a key insight. For each section, choose only the statements the case supports. If a choice is not right, the feedback explains why and you can try again. Then use your persona to choose an opening for the call, and finish by comparing your persona with what the case supports.

The company and the people are fictional. This activity is not scored, and nothing you choose or write is stored or sent anywhere unless you save a text file yourself. Allow about 15 minutes.

How to use this version

You can do every stage on paper or in a document. Read the case, then draw a table with five rows: who they are, what they care about, pain points, how to engage them and key insight. For each of the first four rows, choose 3 or 4 statements from the list below that the case supports, and write them in your table. Then read the feedback for every statement and compare it with your choices. If you chose differently, revise your table. After two unsuccessful tries on a section, read its supported statements. Work through the sections in order, as in the interactive version.

The customer case

Read the fictional case of Carla Hernandez, a data analyst at Primer Systems, a company that develops electronic health record software for healthcare networks. Use its five passages as the evidence for this exercise.

Passage 1: Role

In her role, Carla manages large amounts of healthcare data, making sure it is accurate, consistent and usable across several systems. She develops analytical reports that guide internal leadership decisions and show value to external healthcare clients. She also supports compliance efforts, including audits, and makes sure data processes follow strict industry rules. Her work makes her a bridge between the technical side of data management and the broader business goals of the organisation.

Passage 2: Goal

Carla’s main goal is to streamline data analysis and reporting so that Primer Systems can give healthcare providers timely, accurate and actionable insights. She wants to modernise how the company manages and interprets data by reducing inefficiencies and improving automation. By doing this, she hopes to help Primer Systems stand out as a leader in healthcare technology and keep its software competitive and trusted by clients.

Passage 3: The business problem

Outdated and disconnected platforms make it difficult to produce reliable reports quickly. That delays decisions and weakens customer confidence. Carla needs solutions that simplify integration between systems, automate repetitive tasks and give greater security for sensitive patient information. Without these improvements, Primer Systems risks falling behind competitors that offer more efficient, compliant and scalable platforms.

Passage 4: Challenges

Healthcare data is becoming more complex as the company grows, which makes it harder to keep data both accurate and efficient. Strict compliance requirements add pressure, because even small errors can have significant consequences. Budget limits restrict how quickly new technology can be adopted. Teams used to existing workflows resist change, which makes it difficult to put new ways of working in place. Carla has to balance innovation with caution.

Passage 5: The bigger picture

Carla’s role connects to Primer Systems’ larger objectives: expanding the use of its health record platform and improving patient outcomes through better use of data. By keeping data processes efficient, accurate and compliant, she supports the company’s mission to provide trusted, scalable healthcare solutions. Her analytics show clients the value of the platform, so her daily responsibilities line up with the company’s long-term plans for growth and market leadership.

Use your persona

Your discovery call is about to start. Which opening makes the best use of the persona you have just built?

“I’d like to understand how reporting works for your team today. How long does it take to get reports you can trust, and what does a delay mean for decisions?” - Best fit. Feedback: That fits. It starts from the goal and pain point in your persona, invites the buyer to talk and shows that you have prepared.

“Let me start by walking you through everything our platform can do.” - Not the best fit. Feedback: Not quite. This leads with features. Your persona says the buyer cares about goals and problems, so start there.

“Tell me everything about your company.” - Not the best fit. Feedback: Not quite. This is too general. A persona exists so that you can ask something specific and relevant instead.

“I know your budget must be tight, so let me start with a discount.” - Not the best fit. Feedback: Not quite. Budget limits are a pain point in the case, but opening with a discount assumes the answer and skips what the buyer wants to solve.
