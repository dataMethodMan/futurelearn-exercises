        Choose a response strategy

        Complete text version

        Three customers raise concerns. Choose a response framework, explain its fit and prepare a response that listens before claiming to resolve the concern.

        The same words can carry several concerns. Do not diagnose a person’s feelings, budget or technical requirements from a short quote. Both frameworks can include empathy and questions; justify your chosen starting point using the case.

        This ungraded practice checks whether you choose a supported strategy and fill the writing fields. The quality and fit of your response are self-reviewed. No AI dialogue, external submission or real customer testimonial is provided.

        Framework and evidence reminder

        Feel-Felt-Found

        Feel: acknowledge the concern respectfully. Felt: relate a genuinely comparable experience only when you have a verified example. Found: explain the supported finding and its limits. No other-customer outcome is supplied here, so record what evidence is needed rather than inventing one. You can acknowledge and ask while that evidence is unavailable.

        Acknowledge-Ask

        Recognise the concern respectfully, then ask an open question to understand what lies behind it. Listen before offering an explanation or evidence. This includes empathy and can be useful for cost, risk, technical uncertainty or disappointing past experience.

        Choosing with judgement

        The frameworks are guides, not rules that label people as emotional or logical. Choose or combine elements to fit the concern. Reassurance needs relevant evidence; questions should explore rather than lead the customer to a preselected answer.
