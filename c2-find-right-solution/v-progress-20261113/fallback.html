1. Analytics provider

You are speaking with a data-science company supporting retail clients with sales and inventory predictions. Their workloads have grown quickly, and report generation now takes hours instead of minutes.

Customer statement: We can’t keep scaling our servers manually. Our team spends more time managing infrastructure than running models.

Identify the primary business need

  • Shorter report turnaround with less manual capacity work
  • Long-term retention of rarely used reports
  • Restrict who can read prediction reports
  • Show who changed cloud resources

Four initial approaches: reference cards

Investigate the bottleneck; assess compute and configured scaling

Check where report time is spent, then assess suitable compute capacity and scaling policies for the actual jobs. Virtual servers still require software and configuration management.

Move processing steps into event-driven functions

Function code can run in response to events. Suitability depends on job duration, resources, data movement and how the workflow is divided.

Move older data into archival storage

Archive storage is designed for retained data with retrieval patterns and delays that must be considered.

Add resource-activity logging

Configured logs record supported activity and help investigation. Logging is not extra processing capacity.

2. Healthcare records provider

You are meeting with a healthcare company that manages patient records across several clinics. They store data in different systems and must meet strict privacy and compliance standards.

Customer statement: We must control who accesses patient information and prove compliance during audits. Our current setup makes that nearly impossible.

Identify the primary business need

  • Controlled access with evidence for audits
  • Faster global delivery of public content
  • Restore lost or damaged records
  • Build dashboards of clinic performance

Four initial approaches: reference cards

Review identity, permissions and audit evidence across systems

Identify who needs which access, map cloud and application permissions, and plan evidence of controls. Cloud identity permissions do not automatically govern every application-level record.

Plan encryption and key management

Encryption protects data under configured keys and access policies. It complements access control and evidence planning.

Configure secure content delivery

Content delivery can include encrypted connections, restricted content and other security features. It is not a complete patient-record permission model.

Centralise backup and recovery

Backup policies and tested recovery support data restoration. Backup access also needs controls.

Primary need: Restore lost or damaged records. Recovery is important, but the call asks who can access records and how that is evidenced, rather than describing a loss or restore problem.

3. Seasonal campaign agency

You are meeting with a digital marketing agency that runs seasonal ad campaigns. Their website traffic triples during major promotions, causing unpredictable infrastructure costs.

Customer statement: Our costs spike every time we launch a new campaign. We need stable performance, but can’t keep overspending to handle short-term demand.

Identify the primary business need

  • Respond to short demand peaks with cost visibility
  • Add persistent storage volumes
  • Record configuration changes
  • Recreate environments from templates

Four initial approaches: reference cards

Assess policy-driven scaling with limits and cost monitoring

Configure demand signals, capacity bounds and scaling policies, and monitor cost and performance. Capacity limits can also limit performance.

Track configuration changes

Record resource settings and changes so teams can review their environment.

Add persistent storage volumes

Attach persistent storage where a workload needs it. Capacity, performance and cost depend on the chosen design.

Automate resource provisioning with templates

Templates make resource creation repeatable and can include resources that support scaling. They are not a demand-based scaling policy by themselves.
