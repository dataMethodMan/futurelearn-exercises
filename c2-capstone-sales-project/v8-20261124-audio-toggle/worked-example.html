Buyer roles and responsibilities
A useful stakeholder map connects evidence to engagement. Seniority alone does not establish signing authority, and the learner/facilitator perspective is necessary to a workable plan. Treat an influence assessment as a judgement to support and confirm.
Nina Okafor - Director of IT
Ashbourne wants to scale its learning operations while improving reporting and keeping integration secure and costs understandable. The conversation establishes these priorities, but not the required architecture or buying authority; the biggest open question is which integration and security constraints would rule out an approach. I would first propose a short joint discussion with Nina and Marcus to test technical constraints alongside the cost evidence needed, asking both to confirm who else must participate. If an urgent operational dependency emerges from Sofia, I would change that sequence. Nina leads the technical evaluation of modernization needs, including security, integration and long-term strategy. Her concerns make her an important technical contributor; final investment approval is not established. I would confirm her decision role and prepare migration/data-flow questions with a solutions architect.

Marcus Lee - Finance Manager
Marcus evaluates budgets, total cost and return. I would engage him on current costs, usage assumptions and annual scenarios. His budget-assessment role is known; procurement and approval authority remain to confirm. A useful next step is agreeing the inputs and uncertainty to show in a cost comparison. The case says he evaluates total cost of ownership and return on investment, so I would seek his input on comparison assumptions; that evidence does not confirm spending approval rights.

Sofia Martinez - Operations Lead
Sofia coordinates regional delivery and describes reporting that takes several days because formats differ. She contributes essential workflow evidence. Her implementation insight is important; the case does not establish low influence or final decision rights. I would confirm her part in acceptance and adoption. Tailored cloud value statements
The three statements cover all four role perspectives. Technical value addresses Nina’s integration/security concern; financial value supports Marcus’s evidence-based comparison; operational and learner value connects Sofia’s workflows with Aisha’s engagement and impact needs. Different sound wording is welcome.
Value for Nina: secure modernization and integration
A suitably designed cloud platform could support growth and integration while addressing your security requirements. We would assess data flows, access controls and migration options against your HR/CRM and learner-access needs. Agree performance and integration acceptance criteria before claiming a successful design or transition. Source checked: Google Cloud, “IAM overview” (Google Cloud documentation > Identity and Access Management > IAM overview; accessed 23 November 2026). IAM uses principals, roles and resources to manage permissions for Google Cloud resources. This supports discussing access control; it does not show that Ashbourne’s HR/CRM systems integrate securely or that its compliance needs are met. Nina and the technical teammate would need to confirm the identity model, interfaces and required controls before recommending a design. This is an illustrative capability source, not a provider recommendation. A proposed measure is the proportion of agreed test transactions that transfer correctly under the required access controls; first ask Nina what happens today and agree the acceptance threshold with the specialist.

Value for Marcus: cost visibility and comparison
A transparent model of usage, service and operating costs could help you compare modernization options with your current baseline. We would agree usage ranges, assumptions and costs to include, then show annual scenarios and uncertainty. This supports a better-informed decision without assuming that usage-based pricing automatically improves predictability or lowers spending. A proposed measure is the annual total-cost range under agreed low, expected and high usage assumptions. First ask Marcus for the current annual baseline, included cost categories and the acceptable uncertainty range.

Value for Sofia and Aisha: reporting and learner insight
Consistent reporting inputs and suitable analytics could reduce manual compilation and make learner-progress information more useful across regions. Sofia can help define reporting workflow and effort, while Aisha defines the insight and intervention timing learners and facilitators need. We would measure the current process and agree useful freshness, quality and access criteria before promising real-time reporting or improved learning outcomes.

Joint technical follow-up call plan
The follow-up connects the whole plan: stakeholder responsibilities shape the questions, value statements suggest measures, and objection responses identify evidence to gather. A useful agenda leaves room for customer correction and unknowns. Neither the meeting nor a proposed demonstration proves a technical outcome.
Your proposed call objective
Confirm the priority modernization requirements and agree what evidence is needed to assess migration/integration, useful reporting and annual cost scenarios. The intended outcome is a shared scope for further validation, not an approved architecture or purchase commitment.

Participants and primary contributions
Proposed participants: Nina for IT/security/integration, Marcus for cost inputs and evaluation, Sofia for regional workflows, Aisha for learner/facilitator insight, me as sales representative, and a solutions architect for technical assessment. I coordinate the business context and agenda; the architect leads technical inquiry. Confirm attendance, decision roles and any additional specialists needed.

Your agenda and handoff
Begin by asking each customer participant to confirm their priority requirement and success criteria. Recap the scoping evidence, then invite the architect to explore migration and HR/CRM integration requirements. Review reporting workflows, data quality and useful update frequency with Sofia and Aisha. Review usage/cost assumptions with Marcus, capture unanswered questions, and agree a bounded validation step. Proposed handoff: “Nina, you raised secure HR and CRM integration. With your agreement, I’ll ask our technical teammate to help us identify the interfaces, access controls and evidence we need before suggesting a design. Is that the most useful place to start?”

Proposed follow-up actions, owners and timing to agree
Propose that I circulate the agenda and consolidate questions; Nina and the architect confirm system/data-flow information; Marcus validates baseline cost inputs; Sofia and Aisha define workflow and reporting acceptance needs. Agree the owners, dependencies and review date together. A migration timeline, cost comparison and any demonstration remain drafts until the required information and scope are confirmed.
