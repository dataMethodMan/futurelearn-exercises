Customer case and goals

Ashbourne Global is a mid-sized professional services company that provides human capital solutions and leadership development programs to international non-profits and corporations. The organization delivers most of its programs through a client learning platform that hosts digital courses, assessments, and resources.

The leadership team is now exploring cloud capabilities to modernize this platform and improve program analytics. The current, on-premises system makes it difficult to track learner progress across regions and customer accounts. Reporting is manual and time-consuming, and the platform cannot easily scale for large client programs or new content formats. These limitations impact the company’s ability to deliver personalized learning experiences and demonstrate its impact on customers.

Customer goals to investigate, not verified results:

  • Support more learners without performance issues.
  • Provide real-time dashboards for program outcomes.
  • Integrate securely with existing HR and CRM systems.
  • Provide predictable and transparent costs for long-term planning.

Nina Okafor - Director of IT

Oversees system modernization and is responsible for security, integration, and long-term technical strategy.

Marcus Lee - Finance Manager

Manages budgets and evaluates total cost of ownership and return on investment.

Sofia Martinez - Operations Lead

Coordinates program delivery across regions and needs better visibility into performance and capacity.

Aisha Bello - Learning Manager

Represents facilitators and learners, focusing on engagement, completion rates, and learner feedback.

Scoping conversation

This fictional conversation is an early scoping call. The next meeting is a proposed technical follow-up. Use the customer statements as evidence; the seller’s proposals still require validation. Treat your plan as preparation rather than a completed sale.

Opening

You (Sales representative): Thank you for meeting with me today. I’d like to learn more about your modernization priorities and understand what matters most as you explore options for your learning platform.

Nina (Director of IT): Thanks for joining us. We’ve outgrown our current system, and we need to make some decisions quickly. I’m particularly focused on security, integration, and ensuring that whatever we choose can scale without creating technical debt.

Understanding the current challenges

You (Sales representative): Of course. Can you share what’s creating the most pressure right now?

Sofia (Operations Lead): Operationally, reporting has become a bottleneck. Each region uses a slightly different data format, so compiling program performance takes several days. We need better visibility and more consistent dashboards.

Aisha (Learning Manager): From the learner's perspective, we can’t establish engagement patterns in real-time. If someone falls behind in a leadership program, we typically only find out after the course has ended. We want insights that let us intervene sooner.

Marcus (Finance Manager): For me, predictability is key. Our on-premises environment incurs irregular costs, including hardware, maintenance, and upgrades. We need a clearer idea of long-term spending and ROI.

Future goals

You (Sales representative): It sounds like the modernization initiative needs to support growth, visibility, and consistent outcomes. What does success look like for your team?

Nina (Director of IT): A secure, cloud-based platform that integrates with our HR and CRM systems. We don’t want learners to log into five different tools.

Sofia (Operations Lead): Consistency. If we can standardize reporting and reduce manual work, it frees us to focus on program quality.

Aisha (Learning Manager): Better analytics. We aim to identify which modules have the most significant impact and adjust content accordingly.

Marcus (Finance Manager): And we need a payment model that reflects usage and growth, not significant upfront investments.

Exploring cloud as an option

You (Sales representative): Based on what you’ve shared, cloud capabilities may be worth exploring for scalability, reporting and integration. We would need to validate the design, data and requirements. What concerns should we investigate?

Nina (Director of IT): My main hesitation is migration. Moving years of learner data sounds complicated and risky. How do we know the transition will be secure?

You (Sales representative): I understand why that risk matters. Which data and security requirements should a technical assessment address? We can prepare a session to explore migration and integration options; no comparable customer outcome has been supplied.

Stakeholder objection 1 (Technical risk)

Nina (Director of IT): That helps, but I’d still want to understand exactly how integration with our HR system would work.

You (Sales representative): I can propose a technical session focused on your HR integration requirements and how options would be validated.

Stakeholder objection 2 (Cost and ROI)

Marcus (Finance Manager): My concern is cost. Cloud pricing models look flexible, but I need more clarity on how they translate into yearly spending. I don’t want surprises.

You (Sales representative): I understand the need for clarity. We can examine projected usage, cost components and uncertainty, compare scenarios with your current baseline, and agree what evidence you need. Usage-based pricing alone does not guarantee a predictable annual bill.

Marcus (Finance Manager): Yes, that would help. I want to compare that to what we’re spending on maintaining our current system.

Alignment on engagement needs

You (Sales representative): Aisha, earlier, you mentioned wanting real-time insights. Can you share more about how that affects your team?

Aisha (Learning Manager): When we run programs for international clients, they expect impact reporting to be provided immediately. Right now, we can’t provide that. Better analytics would help us show how learners progress and where they need support.

Sofia (Operations Lead): And from operations, that kind of visibility would let us adjust scheduling and resources faster.

Next steps and joint technical call

You (Sales representative): A proposed next step is a joint call with a solutions architect. We can prepare to assess the following topics and agree what needs further evidence:

  • Migration and integration options
  • Reporting needs and required update frequency
  • Cost scenarios based on projected usage and stated assumptions
  • Detailed technical questions and validation steps

Nina (Director of IT): That would be useful for me. I want to understand exactly what the migration timeline could look like.

Marcus (Finance Manager): And I’d appreciate seeing a clearer view of cost scenarios.

Aisha (Learning Manager): I’d like to review the analytics dashboard if possible.

Sofia (Operations Lead): Yes, I want to ensure it supports regional workflows.

You (Sales representative): Let’s agree the session arrangements and share a proposed agenda in advance so everyone can prepare.
