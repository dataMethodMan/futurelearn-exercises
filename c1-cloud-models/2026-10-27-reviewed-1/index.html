        Who looks after what? - complete text version

        The three main cloud service models are Infrastructure as a Service, or IaaS, Platform as a Service, or PaaS, and Software as a Service, or SaaS. Each one is a different balance of control, convenience and responsibility. In this activity you will read six short descriptions of cloud tools and services. For each one, you will work out who looks after what, the provider or the customer’s own team, and then choose the service model it fits. You will finish by matching each model to the people who use it.

        For each description, first decide who looks after each of three layers. The pattern you find points to the model. Then choose the model. If a choice is not right, the feedback explains why and you can try again. At the end, match the three models to their typical users.

        The businesses are fictional. This activity is not scored, and nothing you choose or write is stored or sent anywhere unless you save a text file yourself. Allow about 20 minutes.

        How to use this version

        You can do every stage on paper or in a document. Read each description, decide for each of the three layers whether the provider or the customer’s own team looks after it, then choose the service model. Read the feedback for every option and compare it with your choices. If you chose differently, work out why before moving on. At the end, match the three models to their typical users.

        The three service models

        The three service models, and what each one means. Keep these in mind as you read.

        Infrastructure as a Service, or IaaS: The provider supplies virtual servers and storage. The customer’s team manages the operating systems, software and security settings.

        Platform as a Service, or PaaS: The provider supplies a managed platform, with the servers, tools and environment already set up. Developers write, test and run their own applications on it.

        Software as a Service, or SaaS: The provider supplies a finished application over the internet and operates the underlying service. Customers use the application while still managing their own data, identities, access and configuration within it.

        To tell the models apart, ask who operates each of three service layers.
