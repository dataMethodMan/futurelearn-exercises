The case

You are a sales representative for Anarres Systems, a cybersecurity startup that serves emerging tech startups. Solitude Innovations builds artificial intelligence (AI) analytics tools and is preparing a new release for larger customers. It needs stronger security and audit readiness. Your company offers a platform with threat monitoring and access controls that can be embedded into their system. The timeline is tight, and the budget is under review. Map the stakeholders, their goals and concerns, and the order in which you would engage them.

Role and rating criteria

Decision-maker: approves major changes in this case. Influencer: shapes the plan or selection. Blocker: can delay progress if risk, governance or commercial conditions are unresolved. End user: works with the tools and experiences their daily impact.

These are primary roles for this exercise, not fixed personality types. A stakeholder may also influence others. Addressing a governance concern is part of building a sound plan.

Use these rating criteria for this exercise. Influence: High means authority to approve or delay approval; Medium means responsibility for shaping tool selection and rollout without final approval; Low means contributing user feedback without approval, budget or rollout authority. Interest: High means direct responsibility for enterprise security and growth, operational delivery, or daily tool use; Medium means financial governance and commercial review rather than those direct responsibilities; Low means no stated involvement or affected goal. These criteria make this case a checked practice task; real account maps need confirmation and may use different scales.

An engagement order is a proposed sequence of purposeful conversations, not a rule to withhold information from people until their turn. You may need an early sponsor check and a later approval discussion with the same person. This map records one main conversation per stakeholder.

How to complete the activity

Work through Maria, Kevin, Jada and Leo in that order. For each person, choose a primary role, influence and interest; write reasons for the ratings, an engagement position and its purpose, a strategy and its purpose, and a question or gap to confirm. Role choices: Decision-maker, Influencer, Blocker, End user. Rating choices: High, Medium, Low. Use engagement positions 1 to 4 once each across your final map.

One possible engagement plan

The checked roles and ratings follow the criteria. This conversation order is an example rather than a required answer. Explain your own plan.

1. Kevin Tan

Clarify technical feasibility, rollout risks and implementation needs before building the wider proposal.

Strategy: Review a rollout plan and ask how to limit disruption to uptime, monitoring and integrations. The conversation addresses his team's delivery responsibilities and stated concerns.

Question to confirm: What implementation timing, dependencies and rollback approach would the team need to confirm?

2. Jada Singh

Check budget controls, return on investment evidence and contract terms early enough to address commercial risks.

Strategy: Discuss a cost and savings summary, spending limits, safeguards and the terms she needs to review. These are the financial conditions and information needs stated in the case.

Question to confirm: Which spending limits, return on investment evidence and contract terms still need agreement?

3. Leo Chen

Gather practical user evidence to test the emerging plan before a final approval conversation. Earlier user input can also be reasonable if you explain that choice.

Strategy: Offer a focused demonstration and ask Leo to test workflow speed, self-service and incident handling. This connects the evaluation to the daily work and productivity concerns he describes.

Question to confirm: Which developer workflows and incident-handling needs should the team validate?

4. Maria Lopez

Use a later approval discussion to present a case supported by technical, financial and user input. This does not mean excluding Maria from early alignment or an initial sponsor check.

Strategy: Present a clear plan connecting risk mitigation, security and a path to scale. It addresses the conditions she sets for approval.

Question to confirm: Which risk evidence and scaling criteria would Maria need before approving a major change?
